# Scrum Master - Vinz Eulo A. Solano

For our final project, I took on the role of Scrum Master for the first time. My job wasn't just to work on the code, but to make sure everyone else could do their jobs without getting stuck. Our team had four different roles: a UI/UX Designer, a Fullstack Developer, a QA & Documentation member, and a Knowledge Management Analyst.

At first, it was a bit overwhelming to coordinate four people with such different tasks. I had to learn how to check in on the design progress so the developer had what they needed, while also making sure our documentation stayed up to date as we made changes.

Since this was a major project, we used a Kanban board to keep track of everything. It was simple: we had columns for what needed to be done, what was in progress, and what was finished. Whenever someone finished a task or got stuck, they updated the board. This helped me see exactly where we were without having to ask everyone for updates every hour.

We also used GitHub to manage our code. Seeing the commits and pull requests helped me understand the technical progress of the "Lost & Found" system. It made it easier to see if we were on schedule to meet our final deadline.

The biggest challenge was moving from just being a student who completes assignments to someone who manages a team’s workflow. I realized that being a Scrum Master means being a problem solver. If the developer didn’t understand a design, or if the QA found a bug that needed fixing before the documentation could be finished, I had to help them communicate and find a solution.

I also learned that clear communication is better than "perfect" planning. In a college project, things change fast. Being able to talk through problems during our quick check-ins helped us stay on the same page.

This project taught me that teamwork is about more than just splitting up the work. It’s about how those pieces fit together. Handling the "Lost & Found Knowledge Board" as my first time as Scrum Master was a great learning experience. It showed me how to lead a group and how much easier a project becomes when you use the right tools to stay organized.
